## For Prospective Coaches and Competitors

### Can international students/teams compete at MIT Science Olympiad?

Because our invitational is currently limited to high school teams in the United States registered with SOINC, we are unable to have international students or teams compete, even in an exhibition or guest team capacity. However, our past tournaments’ exams and their solutions are public on our website [archives](https://scioly.mit.edu/archives/).

### Can individuals compete?

All students wishing to compete at our invitational must be part of an SOINC-recognized high school (Division C) team registered with their state.

### How does the registration lottery work?

For information on how team selection for our annual tournament works, please refer to the [Registration page](https://scioly.mit.edu/registration/) of our website.

### What is the cost of attending?

Accepted schools must pay a $200 registration fee for each team. If your team requires partial or full financial aid, please indicate so on your team registration acceptance form response. All other expenses incurred by travel, lodging, etc. are the responsibility of the competing teams.

### When will teams know if they have gotten off of the waitlist?

Due to the unpredictability of registration changes, we are unable to provide an exact timeline on when teams will know if they have gotten off the waitlist. We will notify teams as soon as possible if a spot has opened up for them to be able to compete at our tournament.

## For Prospective Event Supervisors and Volunteers

### What is the difference between volunteering and ESing?

Generally speaking, ESs (Event Supervisors) are responsible for the operation of their respective events on tournament day and communicating with PC members in preparation to run their event. For events with written exams, ESs are expected to write their event’s exam and grade it on tournament day. For events with a build component, ESs will oversee the testing of devices. For events with a lab component, ESs will design the lab activity and set it up on tournament day. This means that ESs will be tasked with preparing for their events in the months leading up to tournament day, such as submitting exam drafts/day-of testing plans and implementing revisions in response to feedback. Event Supervisor applications typically open in August.

The specific responsibilities of volunteers will depend on the discretion of the ES to whom they are assigned. Volunteers may be asked to grade exams, proctor exams or device testing, or be assigned as “runners” who move between events to help with various tasks during tournament day. In any case, volunteers can expect their responsibilities to lie almost entirely within the competition weekend (Friday evening to Sunday morning).

If you are interested in helping with our tournament (in either volunteer or ES capacity), please find the link to join our email list [here](https://scioly.mit.edu/contact-us/) so that you may be included in any future MIT Science Olympiad sign-up forms.

### Who can be an Event Supervisor?

Individuals who are in college or above (including working adults) are eligible to be an Event Supervisor, provided they are not currently affiliated with competing teams; i.e., one cannot currently be an eligible competitor in Division B or C, a parent, or a coach.

For questions regarding the ES selection process, please refer to this blog post [here](https://scioly.mit.edu/blogs/es-selection-statistics-behind-the-scenes/). In short, however, we select for ESs who are timely, well-organized, open to feedback, highly experienced, and capable of challenging competitors. Previous experiences with returning-applicant ESs do affect our considerations in re-selection.

### How is travel reimbursed for event supervisors?

We will cover travel to/from Boston for event supervisors residing out of state. A travel plan form is sent out shortly after ESs accept their role. When ESs book their plane tickets, a travel voucher and receipt should be submitted with a separate travel reimbursement form.

Reimbursements may take a while to process, especially if there are changes to travel plans due to weather or other unpredictable conditions. Please give at least one month after the travel period before contacting us about reimbursements. Note that there is no reimbursement for insurance or Uber/Lyft.

### What is the event supervisor timeline for arrival and preparation until tournament day?

Generally, ESs are expected to arrive on-campus by Friday afternoon, as there is a mandatory ES orientation that takes place Friday evening. However, ESs for certain lab or build events may arrive a day earlier (Thursday night) in order to check on materials and oversee setup needs.

### What are the living accommodations for event supervisors during their stay at MIT?

We provide housing accommodation for Friday and Saturday nights (or Thursday night if needed) with Planning Committee members or other MIT volunteers. This housing may be in MIT dormitories or FSILGs (fraternities, sororities, and independent living groups). Air mattresses are provided for all ESs staying with PC members.

Dinner is provided on Friday and breakfast, lunch, and dinner are provided on Saturday— please have money or food available for any other days of stay.

### How is communication maintained between event supervisors and Planning Committee?

We mainly use Discord to connect everyone in one place. By accepting their role, event supervisors agree to actively check Discord from their acceptance until shortly after the tournament concludes. This Discord server will eventually become competition-wide and include competitors and volunteers; however, prior communications will be hidden from competitor and volunteer viewing.

We will use email to communicate important information that is specific to the ES, e.g. travel.

### Who can volunteer? Can parents, coaches, or high school students volunteer on the day of the tournament?

Individuals who are in college or above (including working adults) are welcome to volunteer on tournament day, provided they are not currently affiliated with competing teams. This means that high school students and parents or coaches of competing students are not permitted to volunteer.

We will send out further volunteering information and the sign-up form closer to the tournament date. In the meantime, if you are interested in helping with our tournament (in either volunteer or ES capacity), please find the link to join our email list [here](https://scioly.mit.edu/contact-us/) so that you may be included in any future MIT Science Olympiad sign-up forms.
